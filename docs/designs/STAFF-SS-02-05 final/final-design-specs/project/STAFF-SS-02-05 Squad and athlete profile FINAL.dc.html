The squad list, and the athlete profile in the version each role sees

Twelve artboards — seven at 1440 × 900 and five at 375 × 812. Nothing is clickable and no hovers or transitions are active. Same club-local instant as the dashboard board: Mon 7 Sept 2026, 07:40 Europe/London, match Sat 12 Sept v Colthorne RFC, squad of 30. The desktop profile is taller than 900px by design, so each desktop frame is the true first screen and the caption under it names the panels that continue below the fold. The phone profile is not: every panel is one tap from the top.

The phone decision

Status at the top, then a jump bar. No panel is more than one tap away

The 5,828px seven-screen scroll is replaced by a 132px status header and a sticky 52px jump bar that names the panel you are in and its place in the role's order, with All panels beside it. Tapping it opens the full list — every panel, 44px a row, with a value beside each name. Nothing slides sideways and no panel name sits off the right edge. Availability moves from y = 2706 to one tap; entries and corrections from y = 5102 to one tap. The panels themselves are unchanged — the same cards as desktop, one column wide.

Withheld is not read-only

Read-only says who can change it. Withheld leaves no trace at all

A panel a role can see but not edit keeps its full content and closes with a line naming the owner — “Set by medical staff”, “Edited by the sport scientist”. Nothing is greyed. A panel a role cannot see is not rendered: no heading, no placeholder, no lock, no “hidden” row, and no count that would only make sense if a diagnosis existed.

One word for no status

“Not recorded” everywhere. “Not set” is retired

The squad list said “Not set” and the dashboard said “not recorded” for the same absence. The dashboard's word wins, because it is the one already used in a sentence — “27 of 30 have a status · 3 not recorded” — and because an athlete's availability is a record, not a setting. It is a neutral pill, never a tone.
