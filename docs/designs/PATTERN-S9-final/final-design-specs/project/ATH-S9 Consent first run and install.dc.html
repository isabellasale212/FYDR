S9 · ATHLETE CONSENT, FIRST RUN, INSTALL TEACHING · STATIC REVIEW BOARD

The flow that has to exist before an athlete’s data lawfully does

Six artboards, athlete app, 375 wide. Nothing is clickable and no hovers or transitions are active. Ashcombe Rugby Club, 30 athletes, Sun 13 September 2026 09:12 Europe/London. Drawn so that either lawful basis fits without redrawing: performance data and health data are separate blocks throughout, and the decision is one control per block rather than one for the pair. Every piece of wording a solicitor must supply is a dashed placeholder carrying its own reference, and all of them are listed by frame at the foot of the board. No legal wording is drafted here.

THE DECISION SCREEN HAS NO HALOED PRIMARY

Equal weight is drawn by removing the halo, not by enlarging the second option

Artboards 3 and 4 are the only frames in the programme with zero haloed actions. Both choices are 56px, both are the accent outline, and they sit in source order agree then decline with the same width. The system’s one-primary rule is a ceiling, not a quota, and a consent screen is the case that needs the floor.

TWO BLOCKS, NOT ONE AGREEMENT

Performance and health are split on every frame, so a separate health decision can attach later

If the basis lands on legitimate interests for performance and explicit consent for health, block one loses its control and keeps its wording, and block two keeps both. No new screen, no relayout — which is the whole reason the blocks are drawn apart while the answer is open.

ARTBOARD 6 IS NOT A NICETY

Without the install screen the notification specification is inert on every iPhone

iOS will not let code trigger the install and will not let permission be requested before it. So the only mechanism available is teaching, and the only place to put the teaching is a screen. It names the exact menu item and shows where the control physically sits.

Two forms of the same words, and no date of birth anywhere

The guardian gets a link to a page, not an invite to an account, because asking a parent to install an athlete app to answer one question loses most of them. What changed, and on which token

A screen may have zero haloed primaries, and the decision screen does

Agree and decline are both the secondary treatment at 56px and full width, stacked in that order. The halo is the system’s device for naming the action of a page, and a consent page must not have one. This is a clarification of the one-primary rule rather than an exception to it: one is the maximum.

both actions --surf + 1px --accent + --accent label at 6.88:1, min-height 56px, width 100% · no --ring-action on frames 3A and 4B

Primary actions are 56px on this board, and 44px is now only for secondary rows

Every committing action here is 56px: set password, read the choice, agree, decline, send the link again, go to Today, not now, submit entry. The 44px minimum still governs steppers, scale targets, the close control and the “not my guardian” row. ATH-ADULT-03 draws its submit at 44px and is now out of step by 12px — a one-line change, listed here so it is not lost.

primary min-height 56px · secondary and targets min-height 44px · ATH-ADULT-03 footer Button to raise from 44 to 56

Legal placeholders are a drawn component, not grey boxes

A dashed line on the well fill, a monospace reference, and one sentence saying who supplies it and roughly how long it runs. Dashed already means “not this athlete’s own number” on the nutrition board; here it means “not yet written”, and in both cases it means do not read this as final. Sizing the box for the expected length is deliberate — when real wording lands two sentences longer, that is a layout problem found now rather than at handover.

1px dashed --line-dashed on --surf2, --r · reference --font-mono / --t-num-nano / --faint · caption --t-caption / --muted

Password rules are a neutral list before typing, and only the unmet rule takes a tone

Unchecked is an en dash in --faint with the words in --muted, and the frame says a dash means not checked yet. On typing, met rules take a cyan tick and the unmet one takes the bad glyph and --on-bad ink, so the screen never shows three red rows for a password being typed. The count reads 2 of 3 rules met, which is the denominator rule applied to something that is not data.

Frame 3B is the whole answer to “what just happened”: the title states the choice, the eyebrow carries the timestamp, and two cards say what the club sees and what the athlete sees. Nothing appears and then disappears. The decline path also demonstrates the missing-is-not-zero rule from the athlete’s side — their cells read an em dash and the squad denominator drops to 29.

No judgement words, and no data invented

There is no “recommended”, no “most athletes agree”, no “help your coach” and no count of how many teammates have accepted — that last one would be a real number and would also be pressure, so it is left out on both grounds. Every figure on the board is either structural (5 of 5 roles, 2 of 3 rules, 1 of 1 guardian) or already established in the programme (24 of 30 submitted, 30 athletes).

Needs new token

A height for a committing action

The brief’s 44 and 56 are two different jobs and the system names neither. Proposed in the existing style: --tap-min for the floor and --tap-commit for a committing action, so a footer action cannot drift back to 44 by being written by hand. Both are spacing-scale values already in use.

proposed: --tap-min 44px, --tap-commit 56px · sits beside the existing named gaps in tokens/spacing.css

A treatment for wording that is not written yet

Proposed --border-pending, an alias of --line-dashed, and --pending-fill, an alias of --surf2, so the placeholder block is one named pattern rather than a dashed border that could be mistaken for the nutrition board’s club-default treatment. Two aliases, no new values, and the pattern can be found and removed in one search when the wording lands.

proposed aliases: --border-pending → --line-dashed, --pending-fill → --surf2

Still unresolved: a foreground for the accent

Carried forward from ATH-ADULT-03 unchanged. The two haloed actions on this board write #ffffff, which is what the kit Button renders internally, at 8.62:1 on --accent. If --on-accent exists in the app’s own globals.css it should be used; if not, it needs adding.

to confirm in code: --on-accent · board writes #ffffff (8.62:1 on --accent)

Named, not drawn

Withdrawal — Settings › Your data › Data consent

A consent that cannot be withdrawn is not consent, so the row has to exist: one row on the existing athlete settings screen, reading the current state and its date, opening the same two blocks with the opposite pair of actions. Withdrawing changes the athlete’s squad row to “no data consent” with today’s date, stops new entries, and leaves submitted entries alone until the retention wording says otherwise. The club sees the change on the squad list and in the audit trail, and no staff figure is recomputed retrospectively — a mean already published does not move. It is a row, not a frame, and it will not get built unless it is named.

athlete settings row, 44px, chevron · state + date in --muted · deletion behaviour governed by LEGAL-3E

Reachability — a figure on the staff squad view, never merged with compliance

“18 of 30 athletes can receive reminders”, in the shape every other Fydr figure takes, sitting near the compliance figure and never inside it. An install problem is a property of a phone; a missed check-in is a property of a person, and one read as the other produces a coach having a conversation about attitude when the answer was a Home Screen icon. The caption names the cause: 12 have not added Fydr to a Home Screen, of whom 9 are on iPhone. Existing screen, existing figure shape, no new frame.

staff squad view · figure + denominator + caption · never combined with “24 of 30 submitted”

Open against code

1 · Does an invite and password flow exist today, or is account creation staff side only

Artboard 1 assumes a tokenised link with an expiry and a password set by the athlete. If accounts are created staff side with a password handed over, the whole board’s first frame is wrong and the consent decision is being made on an account the athlete did not open — which is a legal problem before it is a design one. Needed: whether an invite token exists, its expiry, and whether the athlete has ever set their own password.

unverified · blocks artboard 1

2 · Is there any field recording a consent decision, its date, and its version

Three values, not one: the answer, the timestamp, and the version of the wording answered. Without the version, a later change to the wording silently rewrites what every existing athlete agreed to. If the field does not exist it is a schema change, and the two blocks need two records, not one, so that a separate health decision can land without a migration.

unverified · two records, one per block · answer + timestamp + version

3 · Is a guardian contact held anywhere for an athlete under 18

Artboard 4 shows a name and a masked address that the club already holds. If no such field exists, the guardian route has no starting point and the club has to be asked for it — which is a staff-side screen this board does not draw and the programme has not specified. Also needed: whether the app knows which athletes are under 18 without displaying a birth date.

unverified · blocks artboard 4 · may require a staff-side capture screen, not drawn

4 · Can an account exist in a declined state, or does every account assume consent

Frame 3B needs an account that is in a squad, appears in lists, and has no entries and no readiness — and needs every staff aggregate to drop it from the denominator rather than count it as a non-submitter. If consent is assumed by the existence of a row, declining is currently indistinguishable from not having answered, and that is the single biggest thing to confirm on this board.

unverified · blocks artboard 3B · affects every denominator in the staff app

5 · Can the app tell whether it is running installed, and can it count that per athlete

The reachability figure needs both: a display-mode check on the client, and a stored per-athlete state for the staff view, refreshed on each open. Without the second, “18 of 30 can receive reminders” cannot be drawn honestly and artboard 6 has no measurable effect. Also open: how far back the iOS Safari toolbar layout in artboard 6 holds, and what the card should say on Android, where the browser can offer the install itself.

unverified · display-mode standalone check · per-athlete stored state · Android path not drawn
