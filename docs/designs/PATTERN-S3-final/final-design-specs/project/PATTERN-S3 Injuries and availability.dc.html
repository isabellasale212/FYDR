The gap this closes

The athlete is told once, and the telling stays until they read it

A status change writes one card at the top of Today — “Your status has changed” — above To do, carrying the new status word and who set it. It is not a toast and it does not expire on a timer: it stays on every Today until the status screen has been opened once, then it is gone and the availability card carries the status as before. Opening it is the only thing that clears it.

The stage decision

Fydr holds the stage number. The club's protocol holds the stage

The only stage data in the captures is “stage 3 of 6”. So the ladder is numbered, not named: the current stage carries what is actually recorded against it — the restriction line, the review date, the expected return — and every other stage is a number and a state word. What the next stage needs is not invented; the screen says the protocol is the source and asks the medic to confirm they have read it.

Two records, never one

An absence is the coach's. An injury is the medic's

The coach's absence form has no clinical half and says so: no diagnosis field, no site, no stage, no injury board row, and a well naming what it does not create. It shares the availability words and tones with the medic's record because a coach reading a squad list must not have to tell them apart, and it shares nothing else.
